Smart camp

This is a guide, not a strict set of rules.

Track to know how much you need.
Exercise to stay strong.
Eat according to your activity levels.
Create a lifestyle that works for you.

TLDR:
Walk every day, track your meals, drinks lots of water and cook without oil.

Tracking

If you have the data, you can make informed decisions.
To properly track, you will need the following:

Hardware:
- Fitness tracker (ex: Fitbit, Apple Watch)
- Body weight scale
- Kitchen scale

Software:
- Yazio
- Google/Apple Health
- Yomi (coming soon)

1) Calculate your range

Calculate your healthy weight range and aim to be in the middle of your BMI: https://www.calculator.net/ideal-weight-calculator.html

My range is from 45.6 - 61.6 kg, the middle would be 53.6kg. I never want to go below 50kg but I want to be slightly under the middle. This way I can go feral on vacation.

2) Find out your base budget

In Yazio:

1. Go to Profile > Gear icon > My Goals
Set your goal weight and switch your activity level to Low.

2. Connect your activity tracker
(double check info is not duplicating with Health connect)

This will make Yazio calculate your calories for losing weight while having no activities. If you switch your goal to maintain weight it will give your BMR (base metabolic rate). Activities from your tracker are then accurately added on top and are not counted twice.

This number is stupid. For me to lose weight without moving it’s a stupid 1165 calories per day. Stay with me. It will make sense in the next step when I talk about exercise.

3) Track your progress

Don't go crazy here. Track twice per week after you wake up and go to the bathroom. Remember things like sleep and stress can influence your weight. Focus on trends.

I call this a "blue sky" reading, if not everything comes out then it’s a "cloudy" reading. TMI but it does move the scale.

Exercise

Now that you've synced your fitness tracker and Yazio, your calorie budget will adjust depending on your daily activity levels. If you move a lot, you will need more calories. The opposite is true for a a relaxed day.
With this type of tracking you avoid stupid rules of having a set calorie limit.

Sadly, you can't change your BMR too much. You can, however, add movement to your routine to increase your calorie budget.

1) Pick the right workout

Choose activities you like to do.

Let's breakdown some examples ~40 minutes of x activity will burn for me:
- Walking ~220 cal
- Strength training ~120 cal
- Pilates ~160 cal
- Running ~400 cal
- Cycling ~220 cal
- Dancing ~250 cal

Strength training is good and helps develop muscles but it doesn't burn that many calories in comparison to cardio. Pilates is similar.
Running burns a lot but it is very tiring and I'm starving afterwards. It works only if I'm short on time.
Cycling depends on how fast you go but it's an efficent way to move around the city.
Dancing is pretty fun and burns good calories.
For me, walking is the best. Low-impact, no preparation needed. Just wake up and go.

2) Develop a sustainable routine

Any type of movement is going to burn calories. Most of us have sedentary jobs but our bodies as designed to move. Start the day with some gentle movement, take walking breaks and sprinkle some strength sessions. Sneak in activites as much as you can.

I like to start the day with a walk after breakfast. If it's cold or raining, dancing is a great second choice. While I work, I do a couple of treadmill walks. If I don't have meetings I go into the city to work from a coffee shop. In the evenings it's strength training or just another walk depending on how I'm feeling.

Food

There are no forbidden foods. However, while you can eat only one pizza per day and still lose weight, the best way is to eat mostly healthy meals and leave a bit of room for fun.

1) Stock your pantry

Buy cheap, nutritious and quality ingredients.

Suggestions:

- Veggies:
Everything, have fun

- Fruits:
Berries, Apples, whatever you want just watch high sugar ones like banana, mango and dates

- Proteins:
Chicken, especially chicken breast, Lean ground Beef, Rump steak, Lentils + Chickpeas, Canned tuna, Canned Sardines, Frozen fish, Eggs, Skyr, Light feta, Exquiza cream cheese, Light mozzarella

- Carbs:
Rice, Pasta, Bread, Potatoes, Corn - just portion according to your needs
I aim to eat around ~180-200 calories worth of carbs per meal (50g rice, 50g pasta, 180g baked sweet potato)

2) Eat balanced meals

Your meals should be a carb, a protein and veggies. This doesn’t have to be boring and you dont need 500 different recipes. You just need a reliable formula: a good sauce, rice plus chicken and veggies.
Yomi for inspo coming soon.

3) Track your food

Eat whatever you want, but track it.
Use the scale and input food into Yazio. If you are eating out, use the AI photo thingie. Not super precise but it gets the job done.

Your calories needs might be different than mine. You can use my numbers as a base and adjust depending on how many calories you need.

4) Make small adjustments

You can make small edits on how you cook and eat that have a big impact:

Cooking without oil will reduce calories by a lot. You can cook the meat in a sauce or bake it, if you pan fry just a bit of water always.

Add veggies or eat fruits as dessert to fill up. If you just wanna snack forever and not really care, try berries.

Dont drink your calories, opt for calorie free or water with fruits. (Try adding frozen berries)

If you eat vegetarian, always supplement protein with high protein puddings (Lidl's milbona schoko pudding is my fav).

5) Share the main dish

What happens if you eat with a partner?
If you share meals with someone who has different calorie needs, you can cook the same dish and adjust in the carbs.

Big G needs a lot more food than me. Without movement my needs are ~600 calories lower. Nature is stupid and I can’t live off of bread.

In practice: I usually eat 50g of oats and he does 100g. Same for rice or pasta. Plus he adds more cheese to things when needed.

Lifestyle

Listen to your body and enjoy your life.

1) Work with your body

Your period will change you. You can gain up to 2kg around it. For those days when you are extra tired and need to eat more, switch your goal to maintenance and aim to do lighter movement like yoga or shorter walks. No need to torture yourself. Take the ibupofren.

I also use this time to draw, read and do spa things. I'm not good at resting so now I have to try extra hard to be nice to my body.

2) Understand your needs

Don't stay hungry but pay attention to what your body really needs. General hunger can mean you didn't drink enough water. Craving sweets is a call for energy, so opt for fruits. Next time you crave something, just do a quick search and try to opt for the healthier version. But if really you want a bit of cake, get the cake.

Other tips: if I know I ate enough I'll try some tea or a bit soda first. If I'm still hungry afterwards, it's time for a snack.

Even when I'm cutting I don't restrict pastries. I just walk more. It balances out.

3) Plan but don’t sacrifice

It's okay to have large meals or go out with friends and have a drink, just track and plan your other meals around it.

If I'm eating pizza for dinner, I'll have skyr for breakfast and aim for a lighter chicken lunch. Add lots of walking and I can go feral.

If I'm short on time and I know my day will have no healthy meals (when my parents visited) I would go for a run. Not my fav but works great while traveling.

4) Get your team onboard

If you eat with a partner or with friends- let them know you wanna opt for healthier meals. Together we can cook at home or go for picnics and choose healthier stuff.

5) Put everything together
